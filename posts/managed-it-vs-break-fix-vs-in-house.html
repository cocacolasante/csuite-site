By Anthony Colasante, Founder, CSuite Code

There are three ways a small business can handle IT: break-fix (pay per incident), managed IT (flat monthly fee, proactive), and in-house (hire your own staff). For most businesses between 5 and 50 employees, managed IT wins on cost, coverage, and risk. Here is how the three models actually compare - and when each one is the right call.

What is the difference between break-fix and managed IT?

Break-fix means you call someone when something breaks and pay by the hour to fix it - reactive by definition. Managed IT means a provider proactively monitors, patches, and secures your systems for a flat monthly fee, so most problems are prevented before they cause downtime. Break-fix is cheaper when nothing goes wrong; managed IT is cheaper the moment something does.

When does break-fix make sense?

Break-fix works for very small offices - roughly one to five people - with simple needs, no compliance obligations, and a high tolerance for the occasional bad day. If a half-day outage would not seriously hurt you and you rarely have problems, paying only when you need help can be the cheapest option. The risk is that break-fix providers are incentivized by problems, not uptime, and you are last in line during a widespread event like a regional outage or ransomware wave.

When is managed IT the right choice?

Managed IT is the sweet spot for most businesses with roughly 5 to 50 employees. You get proactive monitoring, security, and a predictable budget, and the provider is incentivized to prevent problems because more incidents cost them, not you. It is especially compelling if you have any compliance exposure (HIPAA, SOC 2), handle sensitive client data, or simply cannot afford the downtime that comes with waiting to react.

When should you hire in-house IT?

An in-house hire starts to make financial sense past about 50 employees, or when you have constant, business-hours, hands-on needs that justify a full salary. Even then, many growing firms run a hybrid: an internal person for daily floor support plus a managed provider for after-hours coverage, security, and specialized expertise one person cannot cover alone.

Which IT model is cheapest?

It depends entirely on how often things break. Break-fix is cheapest if nothing ever goes wrong - but that is a bet, not a plan. Across a typical year with normal incident rates, managed IT usually comes out lowest for businesses past a handful of employees, because a single serious incident (a ransomware recovery, an extended outage, a breach notification) can cost more than a year of managed fees. In-house is the most expensive per dollar of coverage until you reach the scale that keeps a full-time hire genuinely busy.

The bottom line

If you are under five people and rarely have issues, break-fix is fine. If you are between 5 and 50 people, managed IT almost always wins on total cost and risk. If you are past 50, look at in-house - probably alongside a managed provider, not instead of one. The expensive mistake is staying on break-fix out of habit while your business grows past the point where reactive IT can keep up.
